## Purpose
Hands a caller the nine header values of a png_info through pointers it supplies, and warns if the row they describe is too wide to measure; this is how loadPng 0x100145a0 learns the size and format of the image it has just read. Six of the pointers are mandatory and each is tested on its own, with any one null returning 0 without writing anything (0x1006eafc, 0x1006eb06, 0x1006eb10, 0x1006eb1a, 0x1006eb24, 0x1006eb2e, all reaching the `xor eax,eax` at 0x1006ec1f); the three remaining ones, compression_type, filter_type and interlace_type, are optional and tested one by one just before their store (0x1006eb67, 0x1006eb7a, 0x1006eb8d). Width and height are copied as dwords (0x1006eb3c, 0x1006eb47) while the five byte fields at +0x18, +0x19, +0x1a, +0x1b and +0x1c are each widened to a full dword in the caller's buffer (`xor ecx,ecx` + `mov cl,[..]`, stored at 0x1006eb54, 0x1006eb61, 0x1006eb74, 0x1006eb87 and 0x1006eb9a). What the body does next is worth reading twice: the channel count and the width test are computed from the CALLER'S BUFFERS, not from the png_info - *color_type is re-loaded three times (0x1006eb9f, 0x1006ebb0, 0x1006ebcc), *bit_depth once (0x1006ebe1) and *width once (0x1006ec03) - so a caller that passes the same address twice, or that is itself writing those words, changes the arithmetic. The count is the usual one: 1 for colour type 3, 3 when bit 1 is set, 1 otherwise, plus one for bit 2 (0x1006ebd3, `add edx,1` at 0x1006ebd8). The product *bit_depth * channels (0x1006ebe3) is rounded up to whole bytes (0x1006ebed, 0x1006ebf0) and divided into 0x7fffffff (0x1006ebfd); a *width above that quotient gets png_warning 0x10079040 with the module string 0x1011dcf4 (`jbe` at 0x1006ec05, call at 0x1006ec10). The warning does not change the answer: the function returns 1 either way (0x1006ec18), and unlike png_set_IHDR 0x1007dc70 it writes no rowbytes, so the test is advice to the caller and nothing else.
