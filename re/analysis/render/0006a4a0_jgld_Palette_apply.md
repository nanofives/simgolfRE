## Purpose
Builds the two 16-bit lookup tables a Palette keeps for blitting, one in 5/5/5 form and one in 5/6/5 form, from the 256 four-byte entries the object holds at +0xc. It is guarded by a pair of serials: the work is skipped outright when the serial at +0x814 already equals the one at +0x810 (`cmp` at 0x1006a4c9, `jne` at 0x1006a4cf over the `jmp` to the epilogue), and the first thing a rebuild does is copy +0x810 into +0x814 (0x1006a4e2), so the tables are rebuilt once per change of the serial and never twice. The loop runs over exactly 256 entries (0x1006a4fa, `jge` at 0x1006a501) and reads three bytes of each: +0xe is shifted right 3 and masked to 0x1f (0x1006a513, 0x1006a516), +0xd is shifted LEFT 2 and masked to 0x3e0 (0x1006a525, 0x1006a528) and +0xc is shifted left 7 and masked to 0xfc00 (0x1006a53c, 0x1006a53f); the three are ORed and stored as a word at this + i*2 + 0x40c (0x1006a54d). The second table repeats the same three bytes with the 5/6/5 shifts - right 3 and 0x1f (0x1006a561), left 3 and 0x7e0 (0x1006a573), left 8 and 0xf800 (0x1006a58a) - into this + i*2 + 0x60c (0x1006a59b). Two details are the body's and not the format's: the 5/5/5 mask 0xfc00 covers a sixth bit the shift can never set, because the entry byte is zero-extended and 0xff << 7 is 0x7f80, so it is the same as 0x7c00; and the fourth byte of every entry (+0xf) is never read, which is the peFlags of the `PALETTEENTRY m_e[256]` the matched class declares at +0xc (`re/match/jgld_app.cpp` line 70). The entry byte order is fixed by the shifts: +0xc (peRed) reaches the top of both words, +0xd (peGreen) the middle and +0xe (peBlue) the bottom.
