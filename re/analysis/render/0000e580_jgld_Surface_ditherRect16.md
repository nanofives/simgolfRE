## Purpose
Writes one colour over every second pixel of a rectangle of a 16-bit surface, the parity alternating from row to row, and returns 0, or 24 when the format code is neither 0 nor 1. The colour block runs first (bit-31 test 0x1000e5ae, surface palette 0x1000e5c2, global fallback 0x1000e5d6, format switch 0x1000e60f / 0x1000e615) and its unsupported arm leaves at 0x1000e617 for the `err = 0x18` at 0x1000e669, which makes everything after the test at 0x1000e674 be skipped. The colour is masked to 16 bits (0x1000e67d); a null rectangle is replaced by the bounds rectangle of slot +0xd4 (0x1000e689, call 0x1000e695) and otherwise the caller's is copied to a local (0x1000e6c6..0x1000e6d6), then intersected with the clip rectangle of slot +0xcc by IntersectRect 0x10008590 (0x1000e6f5, tested at 0x1000e6ff), and the top-left pixel address of slot +0x1c (0x1000e717) must be non-NULL (0x1000e728). The row loop is the post-decrement at 0x1000e740..0x1000e74e: a row whose remaining count is odd (the signed `h % 2` idiom at 0x1000e75d..0x1000e769, tested at 0x1000e76c) starts one pixel further right (0x1000e771), records that in a flag (0x1000e777) and has one pixel less to write (0x1000e781); the inner loop then stores a word and steps two pixels while the counter stays above zero (0x1000e79b jle, store 0x1000e7a8, `add edx, 4` at 0x1000e7ae, `sub edx, 2` at 0x1000e795). The next row is reached by adding the slot-+0xe0 pitch less w less that flag, doubled (0x1000e7c0..0x1000e7d9), so an odd row's one-pixel head start is paid back and the written pixels of consecutive rows interleave. Slot +0x24 with 1 closes (0x1000e7ed).
