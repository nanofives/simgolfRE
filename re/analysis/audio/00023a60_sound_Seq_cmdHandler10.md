## Purpose
Applies one sequencer command to a range of tracks: it finds the command record whose tag matches the first argument and writes the second argument into every track record of the range the record names. A zero dword at this+0x224 rejects the command with 0x24 (`test eax, eax` / `jne 0x10023a76` at 0x10023a69). The chain starts at this+0x218 and is mirrored into the cursor at this+0x220 (0x10023a7c, 0x10023a7e), which is the only field the search writes; a node carries its successor at +4 and its payload at +8, and the search returns 0x24 as soon as either is null (0x10023a84, 0x10023a8b, 0x10023aab, 0x10023ab2) and stops at the first payload whose dword at +4 equals the tag (`cmp [eax+4], ecx` / `je 0x10023ab6` at 0x10023a91). The payload's range is its dwords at +8 and +0xc (0x10023ac4, 0x10023ac8): a high end below the low end is rejected with 0xa (`jb 0x10023b2e` at 0x10023acd, UNSIGNED) and so is a high end that is not below the track count the sequencer reports through its own vtable slot +0xb4 (`call dword [eax+0xb4]` at 0x10023ad3, with the object still in ecx and nothing pushed, `jae 0x10023b2e` at 0x10023adb). Seq_listGetAt 0x1001d780 is then called for step 0 on the list at this+0x60 (0x10023aea) and, for every step from the low end to the high end (0x10023b01-0x10023b23, UNSIGNED), the record it returns takes the second argument in its dword at +0x45c (0x10023b1a). Returns 0 (0x10023b28). The two result tests around those calls have OPPOSITE senses: the loop writes when the lookup succeeded (`jne 0x10023b20` at 0x10023b14, and Seq_listGetAt returns 0 on success, 0x24 on failure, by `neg` / `sbb` / `and al, 0xdc` / `add eax, 0x24` at 0x1001d7d5-0x1001d7dc), while `or byte [eax+0x38], 8` at 0x10023af7 runs only when the step-0 lookup FAILED (`je 0x10023afb` at 0x10023af1), in which case the out pointer holds 0 and the store faults at address 0x38.
